## 1. Play types by developmental stage

Tested twice on the practice quiz. Not covered in any Explore section — it was Self-Check: Play (section 14), which extraction skips.

| Stage | Play type | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Infant | **Solitary** | Plays alone; interested in own body and simple objects |
| Toddler | **Parallel** | Plays *alongside* other children, not *with* them; no sharing of the activity |
| Preschool | **Associative** | Plays with others in a shared activity, but with no formal organization, rules, or assigned roles |
| School-age | **Cooperative** | Organized, rule-governed, group goals; team sports emerge |
| Adolescent | Competitive / social | Peer group central; team and social activities |

**Exam trap:** parallel play belongs to the **toddler**, not the preschooler. A question describing a 2-year-old expects parallel play.

## 2. Fine and gross motor milestones

Was Self-Check: Fine and Gross Motor Skills (section 7) — skipped, so only height/weight growth survived into the study guide.

| Age | Gross motor | Fine motor |
|---|---|---|
| 2 mo | Lifts head when prone | Hands mostly fisted |
| 4 mo | Rolls front to back; steady head control | Grasps and brings objects to mouth |
| 6 mo | Sits with support; rolls both ways | **Palmar (raking) grasp**; transfers object hand to hand |
| **9 mo** | Sits unsupported; crawls; pulls to stand | **Pincer grasp** — thumb and forefinger; picks up small items like cereal |
| 12 mo | Stands alone; may take first steps | Neat pincer grasp; tries to feed self with spoon |
| 15 mo | Walks well | Builds 2-block tower; scribbles |
| 2 yr | Runs; walks up and down stairs | Builds 6-block tower; turns pages one at a time |
| 3 yr | **Rides a tricycle**; alternates feet on stairs | Copies a circle; feeds self well |
| 4 yr | Hops on one foot | Copies a cross/square; **draws a person with 3+ parts** |
| 5 yr | Skips; jumps rope | Copies a triangle; ties shoes; prints some letters |

**Exam trap:** *pincer grasp* is the classic **9-month** answer. Tricycle is **3 years**, not 2.

## 3. Infant and newborn reflexes

**Zero coverage in any Week 1–3 guide.** The practice quiz asks Babinski directly.

| Reflex | How elicited | Normal response | Disappears |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Babinski** | Stroke lateral sole from heel upward | **Toes fan out and great toe dorsiflexes** (extends upward) | ~12 months |
| Moro (startle) | Sudden jarring or head drop | Symmetric arm abduction and extension, then adduction; fingers form a C | 3–6 months |
| Rooting | Stroke cheek or corner of mouth | Head turns toward stimulus, mouth opens | 3–4 months (awake) |
| Sucking | Touch lips or place object in mouth | Strong, coordinated suck | ~10–12 months |
| Palmar grasp | Place finger in palm | Fingers curl tightly around | 3–4 months |
| Plantar grasp | Press thumb against ball of foot | Toes curl downward | 8–10 months |
| Tonic neck ("fencing") | Turn head to one side while supine | Arm and leg extend on that side, flex on the other | 3–4 months |
| Stepping | Hold upright with soles touching a surface | Alternating stepping movements | ~4 weeks |

**Exam trap:** in an infant, a **positive Babinski (toes fanning) is NORMAL**. Toes curling *downward* is the abnormal finding in this age group — it is the adult-normal response. Persistence of a primitive reflex past its expected disappearance, or asymmetry, suggests a neurologic problem.

## 4. Infant feeding rules

Only one line exists in the Edapt guide (breastfeeding recommended for the first year; iron-fortified cereal). The quiz tests the safety rules.

- **No honey before 12 months** — risk of **infant botulism**. This is the classic "needs further teaching" answer.
- **No cow's milk before 12 months** — poor iron source, risk of GI blood loss and iron-deficiency anemia. Whole milk after 12 months.
- **Introduce solids around 6 months**, when extrusion reflex fades and head control is adequate. **Iron-fortified rice cereal** is the traditional first food.
- Introduce **one new food at a time, waiting 3–5 days** between, to identify allergies.
- Continue breast milk or formula alongside solids through the first year.
- No juice before 12 months; avoid choking hazards (whole grapes, nuts, popcorn, hot dog rounds) in young children.

## 5. Bicycle helmet fit and safety

The Edapt guide notes only that bicycle and motor vehicle accidents are the most common school-age injuries.

- Helmet sits **level on the head, parallel to the floor** — not tilted back off the forehead.
- Front edge roughly **two finger widths above the eyebrows**.
- Straps form a **V under each ear**; chin strap snug — no more than one or two fingers fit under it.
- Must fit **now** — never buy oversized "to grow into."
- Required for **bicycles, skateboards, scooters, and rollerblading**, not bikes alone.
- Replace after any crash, or if cracked or damaged.

## 6. Kohlberg's stages of moral development

Appeared only as Self-Check: Kohlberg's Stages of Moral Development (section 5) — skipped, so there is **no Kohlberg content anywhere in your files**. Not on this practice quiz, but it is course content.

| Level | Stage | Typical age | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Preconventional** | 1. Punishment–obedience | Toddler–preschool | "Is it going to get me punished?" |
| | 2. Instrumental–relativist | Preschool–school-age | "What's in it for me?" — naive reciprocity |
| **Conventional** | 3. Good boy–nice girl | School-age | Seeks approval; conforms to please others |
| | 4. Law and order | School-age–adolescent | Respects authority and fixed rules; duty |
| **Postconventional** | 5. Social contract | Adolescent+ | Rules are agreements that can change for the common good |
| | 6. Universal ethical principle | Adulthood (often never) | Abstract, self-chosen ethical principles |
