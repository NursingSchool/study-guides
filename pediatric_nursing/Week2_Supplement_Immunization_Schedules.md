## Birth through 6 Years

| Vaccine | When given |
|---|---|
| **Hepatitis B** | Dose 1 at **birth**; Dose 2 at **1–2 mo**; Dose 3 at **6–18 mo** |
| **Rotavirus** | Dose 1 at **2 mo**; Dose 2 at **4 mo**; Dose 3 at **6 mo** *(some children only — depends on brand)* |
| **DTaP** | Doses 1–3 at **2, 4, 6 mo**; Dose 4 at **15–18 mo**; Dose 5 at **4–6 yr** |
| **Hib** | Doses 1–2 at **2, 4 mo**; Dose 3 at **6 mo** *(some children only — depends on brand)*; Dose 4 at **12–15 mo** |
| **Pneumococcal (PCV)** | Doses 1–3 at **2, 4, 6 mo**; Dose 4 at **12–15 mo** |
| **Polio (IPV)** | Dose 1 at **2 mo**; Dose 2 at **4 mo**; Dose 3 at **6–18 mo**; Dose 4 at **4–6 yr** |
| **MMR** | Dose 1 at **12–15 mo**; Dose 2 at **4–6 yr** |
| **Chickenpox (Varicella)** | Dose 1 at **12–15 mo**; Dose 2 at **4–6 yr** |
| **Hepatitis A** | **2 doses separated by 6 months**, starting at **12–23 mo** |
| **Influenza** | **Every year from 6 mo**; two doses for some children |
| **RSV antibody** | At birth: depends on mother's RSV vaccine status. Later: depends on child's health status |
| **COVID-19** | Discuss with provider (shared decision-making category) |

**Pattern worth memorizing:** the **2-4-6 month** series is DTaP, Hib, Pneumococcal, Polio, Rotavirus. The **12–15 month** cluster is MMR, Varicella, Hib #4, PCV #4. The **4–6 year** boosters are DTaP #5, Polio #4, MMR #2, Varicella #2.

**Only Hepatitis B is given at birth.**

## 7 through 18 Years

| Vaccine | When given |
|---|---|
| **HPV** | Routine at **11–12 yr**; may start at **9 yr**. 2 or 3 doses |
| **Tdap** | **11–12 yr** (1 dose) |
| **Meningococcal ACWY** | Dose 1 at **11–12 yr**; booster at **16 yr** |
| **Meningococcal B** | **16–18 yr**, shared decision-making with provider |
| **Influenza** | **Every year**; two doses for some children aged 6 mo through 8 yr |
| **COVID-19** | Shared decision-making, all ages in range |
| **Mpox** | **18 yr**, 2 doses |
| **Dengue** | **9–16 yr**, ONLY if living where dengue is common AND lab-confirmed past dengue infection |

Additional Tdap notes from CDC: extra doses if childhood doses were missed; **1 dose for dirty wounds**; **1 dose every pregnancy**.

## Live attenuated vs. inactivated *(added — not on the CDC consumer pages)*

This matters because your Edapt module lists "immunocompromised children cannot receive live virus vaccines" as a contraindication, but never says which ones are live.

- **Live attenuated:** MMR, Varicella (chickenpox), Rotavirus (oral), intranasal influenza (LAIV), MMRV combined
- **Inactivated / subunit / toxoid / mRNA:** Hepatitis B, Hepatitis A, DTaP and Tdap, Hib, Pneumococcal, Polio (IPV in the US), HPV, Meningococcal, injectable influenza (IIV), COVID-19

## Contraindications and reactions (from the Edapt module — cross-reference)

- Severe febrile illness — **minor illness is NOT a contraindication**
- Immunocompromised children cannot receive live virus vaccines
- Known allergic response to a previous dose or a vaccine component

Expected reactions occur hours to days after injection: localized tenderness, redness, swelling at the site, low-grade fever, irritability.

## Diseases prevented and their complications

High-yield because it connects the vaccine to the disease the module already covers in detail (chickenpox, measles, mumps, German measles/rubella, whooping cough/pertussis).

| Disease | Key complications |
|---|---|
| Hepatitis B | Chronic liver infection, liver failure, liver cancer, death |
| Rotavirus | Severe diarrhea, dehydration, death |
| Diphtheria | Swelling of the heart muscle, heart failure, coma, paralysis, death |
| Pertussis (whooping cough) | Pneumonia, death — especially dangerous for babies |
| Tetanus (lockjaw) | Seizures, broken bones, difficulty breathing, death |
| Hib | Brain damage, hearing loss, loss of arm or leg, death |
| Pneumococcal | Pneumonia, blood poisoning, meningitis, death |
| Polio | Paralysis, death |
| Measles (rubeola) | Brain swelling, pneumonia, death |
| Mumps | Brain swelling, painful swollen testicles or ovaries, deafness, death |
| Rubella (German measles) | Very dangerous in pregnancy — miscarriage, stillbirth, premature delivery, severe birth defects |
| Chickenpox (varicella) | Infected sores, brain swelling, pneumonia, death |
| Hepatitis A | Liver failure, death |
| RSV | Pneumonia and bronchiolitis; especially dangerous for infants |
| Influenza | Pneumonia, sinus and ear infections, worsening of heart or lung conditions, death |
| HPV | Genital warts; cancers of the cervix, vagina, penis, anus, throat |
| Meningococcal | Loss of arm or leg, deafness, seizures, death |

**Mnemonic aids:** DTaP protects against **d**iphtheria, **t**etanus, **p**ertussis. MMR protects against **m**easles, **m**umps, **r**ubella.
